# Planet

## Definition
A planet is a large, nearly spherical body that orbits a star and has cleared most other objects from its orbital path. Planets can be rocky, like Earth and Mars, or gas and ice giants, like Jupiter and Neptune. This definition also applies to exoplanets, which orbit other stars and broaden our view of possible planetary systems.

## Main characteristics
Rocky planets have solid surfaces, layered interiors, and atmospheres that range from thin to dense. Gas and ice giants are dominated by hydrogen, helium, and volatile compounds, and they often have strong magnetic fields and complex weather patterns. Key properties include mass, radius, density, atmospheric composition, surface temperature, and orbital parameters. Many planets host moons and rings, which provide clues about their formation and the evolution of their systems.

## Scientific importance
Planets are central to the study of habitability. Comparing atmospheres reveals how climate systems evolve and how water and essential molecules are retained or lost. Exoplanet surveys show the diversity of planetary types and the frequency of Earth sized worlds. Planetary geology and chemistry explain how surfaces change over time, guiding models of planetary evolution and the conditions that support life.

## Relevance for space exploration
Planetary science informs mission design, landing site selection, and resource planning for future exploration. Studying Mars, Venus, and icy moons provides strategies for robotics, sample return, and potential human missions. Understanding atmospheric hazards, radiation environments, and surface conditions improves spacecraft safety and operational planning. Planetary knowledge also supports defense against potential impacts and guides the search for biosignatures.

## Impact on Earth
Planetary research drives advances in robotics, materials, and remote sensing that are used in Earth observation and disaster response. Comparing planetary climates strengthens Earth climate models and improves forecasting. The search for exoplanets accelerates innovation in imaging and signal processing, which benefits communications and medical diagnostics. These efforts also inspire education and public engagement, building skills that support technology and sustainability on Earth.
