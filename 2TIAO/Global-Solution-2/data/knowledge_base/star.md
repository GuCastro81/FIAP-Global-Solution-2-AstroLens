# Star

## Definition
A star is a massive, luminous sphere of plasma held together by gravity and powered by nuclear fusion. In a star's core, hydrogen fuses into helium, releasing energy that produces light and heat. The Sun is our closest star and the main energy source for Earth.

## Main characteristics
Stars are classified by temperature and spectral type, from cool red dwarfs to hot blue giants. A star's mass largely determines its lifetime and fate: low mass stars can burn for billions of years, while massive stars live quickly and end as supernovae. Key properties include luminosity, radius, surface temperature, rotation, and chemical composition. Many stars belong to binary or multiple systems, which can influence orbital dynamics and the formation and stability of planets.

## Scientific importance
Stars are central to understanding nuclear physics and the chemical evolution of the universe. Stellar spectra reveal composition, temperature, and motion, enabling distance measurements and mapping the structure of the Milky Way. Stellar evolution explains how elements like carbon, oxygen, and iron are created and dispersed, which are essential for planets and life. Studying variable stars also provides reliable distance markers that calibrate the cosmic distance ladder.

## Relevance for space exploration
Stars shape the habitability of planetary systems by controlling radiation levels and long term stability. Stellar activity, such as flares and magnetic cycles, affects spacecraft operations and potential biospheres. Navigation systems use star trackers and pulsars for precise orientation. Accurate stellar models help plan deep space missions by improving trajectory predictions and understanding the radiation environment encountered beyond Earth's magnetosphere.

## Impact on Earth
Solar and stellar research improves space weather forecasting, protecting satellites, communications, and power infrastructure. Techniques developed for stellar spectroscopy and photometry translate to remote sensing and medical imaging. Understanding the Sun's energy output provides context for climate studies, helping distinguish natural solar variability from human driven climate change. Education and outreach centered on stars also inspire innovation and strengthen STEM skills across society.
